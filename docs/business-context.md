# Business Context — Travel Marketplace

## 1. Background

The travel business operated in a competitive market where flight bookings represented a significant part of its traditional offering. Customers could compare flight prices across providers or book directly with airlines, creating pressure to diversify the company's value proposition and revenue opportunities.

During a brainstorming session, I championed a discovery discussion to explore how the business could expand beyond flight bookings into a broader travel marketplace.

The opportunity was to bring multiple travel-related products and services together on one platform, creating more value for customers while opening additional revenue opportunities for the business.

## 2. Business Problem

A business heavily dependent on flight bookings faces several challenges:

* Competition from airlines and other travel booking platforms.
* Customer price sensitivity and the ease of comparing flight prices.
* Limited opportunities to generate revenue from complementary travel services.
* The need to attract and retain customers through a broader value proposition.
* The need to diversify product supply and commercial opportunities.

The business therefore needed to explore a model that could support a wider range of travel services rather than relying primarily on flight transactions.

## 3. Business Opportunity

The proposed solution was a travel marketplace where customers could discover and purchase a range of travel products.

Potential marketplace categories included:

* Flights
* Hotels
* Tours and holiday packages
* Car hire
* Airport concierge and protocol services
* Destination activities
* Study programmes
* Other travel-related services

The marketplace concept also created an opportunity to accommodate products from both internal departments and external travel agencies or service providers.

This approach could expand the available product catalogue without requiring the company to own or directly operate every service.

## 4. Strategic Question

An important stakeholder discussion concerned the marketplace's operating model.

Should the marketplace feature only products managed by the company, or should external travel agencies and providers also be allowed to participate?

An internal-only model would provide greater direct control over the catalogue but could limit the breadth of available products.

An open marketplace could broaden product selection and supplier participation, but it would require clear governance, product approval, vendor accountability, and consistent reporting.

The proposed direction was to accommodate both internal and external products under a shared marketplace framework.

## 5. Proposed Business Solution

The marketplace concept brought together five key capabilities.

### 5.1 Product Discovery

Customers could browse product categories, search for relevant services, review product details, and discover products associated with their travel destination.

### 5.2 Vendor Participation

Internal departments and external providers could create and submit products for consideration.

### 5.3 Central Product Governance

A dedicated Marketplace Administrator would review submitted products against marketplace guidelines before they became visible to customers.

### 5.4 Unified Customer Transaction

Customers could select multiple eligible products, add them to a cart, and proceed through the website's checkout and payment process.

### 5.5 Commercial Reporting

Management could assess marketplace performance through revenue, customer growth, vendor growth, sales activity, and return-on-investment indicators.

## 6. Stakeholders

The key stakeholder groups included:

* Customers and travellers
* Internal departments acting as product owners
* External vendors and travel agencies
* Marketplace Administrator
* Finance and operational teams
* Executive management

Each stakeholder group had different responsibilities and information needs, making clear requirements and process ownership important to the design.

## 7. My Role as Business Analyst

My contributions included:

* Championing the discovery discussion around business diversification.
* Facilitating stakeholder conversations about the marketplace operating model.
* Coordinating requirements gathering and sprint planning.
* Proposing a dedicated Marketplace Administrator to support neutral product governance.
* Supporting the definition of business rules, workflows, and user stories.
* Considering the end-to-end customer journey, including discovery, checkout, payment, and fulfilment.
* Coordinating UAT around agreed business requirements.
* Identifying the need for management reporting that distinguished internal product revenue from external vendor revenue.

## 8. Business Objectives

The marketplace was intended to support the following objectives:

1. Reduce reliance on flight bookings as the primary commercial offering.
2. Broaden the range of travel products available to customers.
3. Enable participation by external providers alongside internal product owners.
4. Create opportunities for complementary product discovery and cross-selling.
5. Establish consistent product governance.
6. Support a consolidated online purchase journey.
7. Improve visibility into revenue contribution and marketplace growth.
8. Give management a basis for evaluating marketplace performance and ROI.

These objectives describe the intended business value; they are not claims of quantified results.

## 9. Key Business Analysis Challenge

The central challenge was not simply defining a new website feature. It was translating a diversification opportunity into a workable operating model.

That required consideration of:

* Who could supply products.
* How products would be reviewed and approved.
* How customers would discover and purchase products.
* Who would receive bookings and fulfil orders.
* How cancellations and minimum-participant conditions would be handled.
* How internal and external commercial activity would be reported.
* How management would evaluate business value.

## 10. Key Takeaway

The marketplace concept represented a shift from a predominantly flight-booking business toward a broader travel commerce model.

My Business Analyst contribution focused on connecting the strategic opportunity to practical business processes, stakeholder responsibilities, product requirements, governance, testing, and reporting.

**Core principle:** A successful marketplace must connect customer demand, product supply, governance, transactions, fulfilment, and measurable business value.
