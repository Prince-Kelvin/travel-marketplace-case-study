# Business Context

## 1. Background

The business operated within a highly competitive travel environment where flight bookings represented a significant part of the traditional travel agency model.

Travellers had increasing access to multiple travel agencies, airline websites and online travel platforms, making it easier to compare prices and choose where to make a booking.

This created a commercial challenge: competing primarily on flight bookings could limit differentiation and make revenue increasingly dependent on a highly competitive product category.

The business therefore identified an opportunity to expand beyond traditional flight bookings by creating a broader digital marketplace for travel-related products and services.

---

## 2. Business Problem

The core business problem was not simply the absence of a marketplace.

The broader challenge was:

> **How can the business diversify its travel offering and create additional revenue opportunities while making the digital platform more valuable to travellers and travel service providers?**

Several factors contributed to the problem:

### Increasing competition

The number of travel agencies in the market continued to increase, giving travellers more options when searching for flights and other travel services.

### Price sensitivity

Flight bookings were highly comparable. Travellers could search across different agencies or book directly through airline websites.

### Limited revenue diversification

A business heavily dependent on flight bookings has fewer opportunities to monetise a traveller's broader travel journey.

### Fragmented travel planning

A traveller planning a trip may need several services:

* Flights
* Hotels
* Tours
* Activities
* Airport services
* Car hire
* Holiday packages

These services could traditionally require interaction with different providers or platforms.

The marketplace opportunity was therefore both a **commercial diversification strategy** and a **customer-experience opportunity**.

---

## 3. Strategic Opportunity

The proposed marketplace would extend the existing travel website from primarily supporting travel bookings into a broader platform where customers could discover and purchase multiple travel products.

The marketplace would support:

* Company-owned products.
* Products created by internal departments.
* External travel agencies and travel service providers.
* Partner ecosystem products and distribution opportunities.

The resulting proposition was:

> **Give travellers more reasons to use the platform while giving travel providers another channel through which to reach customers and generate sales.**

---

## 4. Marketplace Hypothesis

The underlying business hypothesis was that an open marketplace could create value for three groups simultaneously.

### Travellers

More travel products and services could be discovered through one platform.

### Vendors

Travel agencies and service providers could gain an additional distribution channel and access to potential customers.

### The Business

The marketplace could create additional revenue opportunities beyond traditional flight bookings while increasing customer engagement with the wider travel platform.

This created a potential marketplace flywheel:

```text
More Vendors
      ↓
More Products
      ↓
More Customer Choice
      ↓
More Customer Interest
      ↓
More Transactions
      ↓
More Vendor Sales
      ↓
More Vendor Interest
      ↓
More Vendors
```

The initial stage would require active vendor acquisition. However, the longer-term objective was to create enough demand and sales activity that vendors would see the marketplace as a valuable channel and seek to participate.

---

## 5. Stakeholder Tension During Discovery

The marketplace concept generated different perspectives among stakeholders.

One perspective was to keep the marketplace focused primarily on products owned or curated by the company.

Another perspective was to open the marketplace to external travel agencies and providers.

The debate raised several business questions:

* Would external vendors create meaningful value?
* How would product quality be controlled?
* Who would approve products?
* How would vendors be attracted to the platform?
* How would products compete for customer attention?
* Who would fulfil customer purchases?
* How could the marketplace generate enough demand to remain attractive to vendors?
* How could the company maintain customer trust while expanding the product ecosystem?

These discussions became an important part of the discovery process.

---

## 6. My Business Analysis Approach

Rather than treating the competing stakeholder opinions as isolated requirements, I approached the discussion by considering the wider business model and operational implications.

I focused on questions such as:

* What problem are we actually trying to solve?
* Who benefits from the marketplace?
* What would make external vendors want to participate?
* How can the marketplace scale beyond company-owned inventory?
* How can the customer experience remain consistent regardless of who owns the product?
* How should products be governed?
* How should vendors be treated fairly?
* What revenue opportunities can be created without compromising marketplace trust?

This led to several product and process decisions that became part of the marketplace design.

---

## 7. Key Decisions Emerging from Discovery

### Open Marketplace

The marketplace would support external vendors rather than functioning exclusively as an internal marketing channel.

### Central Product Governance

A dedicated Marketplace Administrator would review products against approved guidelines before publication.

### Equal Organic Product Treatment

Approved products would receive equal organic marketplace treatment rather than being manually prioritised based on internal preference.

### Paid Visibility

Vendors could voluntarily pay to boost an approved product and obtain higher search placement.

### Unified Customer Commerce

Customers could purchase multiple travel products through a single cart, checkout and payment journey.

### Vendor-Owned Fulfilment

The relevant vendor or internal product-owning department would retain responsibility for fulfilling the purchased service.

### Performance Measurement

Different stakeholder groups would receive different levels of reporting based on their responsibilities.

---

## 8. Marketplace Value Proposition

The marketplace was therefore designed around three connected propositions.

### For Travellers

**One platform for multiple travel needs.**

Travellers could discover and purchase flights, accommodation, tours, holiday packages, airport concierge services, activities and other travel-related products.

### For Vendors

**A digital sales and distribution channel.**

External providers could list products, receive customer orders and fulfil services while gaining access to marketplace customers.

### For the Business

**A broader travel ecosystem and additional revenue opportunity.**

The marketplace could generate value through transactions, vendor participation and paid product visibility while encouraging customers to engage with the wider travel platform.

---

## 9. Success Measures

The business wanted to understand whether the marketplace was generating sufficient commercial value relative to the investment required to build and operate it.

The executive-level measurement framework therefore focused on:

### Revenue

* Revenue from internally uploaded products.
* Revenue generated from external vendors.
* Gross marketplace revenue.

### Customer Growth

* Growth in customers using the platform.
* Customers directed to the main BTM Holidays website.

### Vendor Growth

* Total number of vendors.
* New vendor registrations.
* Growth of the marketplace vendor base.

### Platform Performance

* Overall marketplace sales.
* Vendor performance.
* Destination performance.
* Marketplace return on investment.

These measures were intended to answer a fundamental management question:

> **Is the marketplace generating sufficient commercial value to justify the investment in the platform?**

---

## 10. Business Analysis Outcome

The discovery process transformed the initial idea of an internal travel marketplace into a broader marketplace model with:

* Internal and external product supply.
* Centralised product governance.
* Consistent product treatment.
* Optional paid visibility.
* Unified customer checkout.
* Vendor-owned fulfilment.
* Customer and vendor notifications.
* Capacity and minimum-participant rules.
* Cross-selling opportunities.
* Stakeholder-specific performance reporting.

The resulting product model connected **business strategy, customer experience, vendor participation and operational fulfilment** within a single platform.

---

## 11. BA Perspective

The key lesson from the discovery phase was that the marketplace could not be treated simply as another website feature.

It represented a change in the way the business could create and capture value.

The analysis therefore had to consider not only:

> **"What should the system do?"**

but also:

> **"How should the marketplace operate, who should participate, what incentives should they have, and how does the model create sustainable business value?"**

This broader perspective influenced the requirements, governance model, customer journey, vendor workflow and measurement framework that followed.
